## Context

The forces at play: the requirement, the constraints, and what makes this a decision rather than an
obvious default. Written so someone with no prior knowledge of the project understands why a choice
was needed at all.

## Decision

What was decided, stated in one or two sentences, in the active voice. "We will use X for Y."

## Alternatives considered

Each alternative that was genuinely on the table, with the reason it was not chosen. An ADR listing
only strawmen is worse than no ADR — it makes the decision look unexamined.

Why it was rejected.

## Trade-offs

What is given up. Every real decision costs something; an ADR with no downsides is a decision that
was not thought through.

## Consequences

What becomes true as a result — positive and negative — and what future work this creates or blocks.
Include what would have to happen to reverse this decision, and how expensive that would be.
